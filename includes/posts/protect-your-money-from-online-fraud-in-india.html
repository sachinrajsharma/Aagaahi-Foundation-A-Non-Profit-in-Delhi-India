Online fraud in India costs families real savings every week, and it rarely looks like a scam at first. It looks like a call from your bank, a message about a parcel, or a friendly stranger offering part-time work. The good news is that most scams follow the same few patterns. Once you know them, you can stop almost all of them.

At Aagahi Foundation, we run financial literacy and cyber safety sessions for community members and for police personnel. This guide puts the most useful points into plain language. Share it with someone who does not use the internet much. They need it most.

Five patterns behind most online fraud in India

Scammers change their story, but the structure stays the same. They create a reason to panic or a reason to hope, and then they ask you to act quickly.

  1. The KYC or bank call. Someone says your account will be blocked today unless you "update KYC". They ask for an OTP or a PIN, or they ask you to install an app.
  2. The UPI "collect" trick. You are told money is on its way. Then a request arrives and you are asked to enter your PIN to "receive" it. That one step sends money out.
  3. The fake helpline. You search for a customer care number and click the first result. Scammers sometimes get their own numbers to show up there.
  4. The easy-money offer. Part-time jobs, "tasks" for rewards and guaranteed investment returns all work the same way. A small payout builds trust, and then the big payment disappears.
  5. The fake officer. A caller claims to be from the police, customs or an investigation agency. They say you are under "digital arrest" and must stay on a video call. No real agency arrests anyone over a video call.

Three rules that stop most scams

You do not need technical skill to stay safe. You need a few habits that you follow every time.

Rule 1: Never share an OTP, PIN or CVV

These codes exist so that only you can approve a payment. Your bank, a delivery company or a government office has no reason to ask for them. Government guidance on UPI says the same: share your card number, expiry date, CVV, PIN or OTP with no one. Remember that a UPI PIN is only for sending money.

Rule 2: Pause when someone is in a hurry

Fraud depends on speed. So slow down on purpose. Say "I will call you back" and hang up. A real bank will happily wait ten minutes. A scammer will not.

Rule 3: Call back on a number you trust

Use the number printed on your card, on your passbook or inside your bank's official app. Never use a number that a caller or a search result gave you. For news and safety advice, you can also read the public pages of the Reserve Bank of India and NPCI, which runs UPI.

If you lose money: act in the first hour

Speed matters because the money moves quickly between accounts. If you have been defrauded, do these things in this order.

  1. Call the national cyber crime helpline, 1930, as soon as you can.
  2. Then file a complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. If the helpline is busy, go straight to the portal.
  3. Call your bank, using the number on your card, and ask them to block the transaction and your card or UPI.
  4. Keep every screenshot, message, number and transaction ID. You will need them for the complaint.

Be realistic about the outcome. Reporting early gives you a better chance, but recovery is not guaranteed. That is why prevention matters more than any cure.

Report suspicious calls and messages before you lose money

You can also report a suspicious call, SMS or WhatsApp message before any damage happens. The Department of Telecommunications runs a facility called Chakshu on the Sanchar Saathi portal. You can report communication you received in the last 30 days. However, Chakshu is not for fraud that has already happened. For that, use 1930 and the cyber crime portal.

For students and first-time jobseekers

Young people face a particular kind of scam: the job that is too easy. A message offers good pay for liking videos, rating products or sending a few forms. Then it asks for a small "registration fee", or for a photo of your Aadhaar or PAN card to "verify" you.

  • A genuine employer never charges you a fee to hire you. If money has to leave your account before work begins, walk away.
  • Look for the company's own website, and check that the email on your offer letter matches its domain.
  • Share identity documents only after you have met the employer or verified them on an official channel. Never send them over a chat app to a stranger.
  • Talk to a teacher or an older relative before you pay anyone. Scammers hate a second opinion.

Money habits that make fraud less painful

Financial literacy and cyber safety go together. A few simple habits limit the damage if something goes wrong.

  • Keep only a small amount in the account you use for UPI. Save the rest in a separate account.
  • Set a daily transaction limit in your bank app, where the app allows it.
  • Check your account statement once a month, and ask about any payment you do not recognise.
  • Use a different password for your email than for any shopping site. Email is the key to your other accounts.
